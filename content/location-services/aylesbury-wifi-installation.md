## Why Aylesbury Homes and Businesses Need Hardwired Wi-Fi

Poor Wi-Fi can have different causes across Aylesbury.

In a newer family home, the broadband router may sit near the incoming fibre point while bedrooms, study spaces and smart-home devices are distributed across several floors. Modern insulation, foil-backed materials and dense construction can also make room-to-room signal less predictable than the floor plan suggests.

In an office, clinic or commercial unit, the problem is often capacity and layout. Staff devices, guest access, VoIP, tablets and cloud systems can all end up competing for a consumer-grade wireless network that was never designed for the site.

A hardwired UniFi design uses Ethernet for the backbone and Wi-Fi for the shorter final connection to devices.

This approach is useful for:

- upstairs and rear-room **Wi-Fi dead zones**;
- home offices with unstable video calls;
- new-build homes where the ISP router does not cover the whole property;
- larger family homes with extensions;
- offices with separate staff and guest requirements;
- clinics and professional premises;
- warehouses or commercial units needing managed wireless zones;
- garden offices and detached workspaces.

Where the wider network and client devices justify it, Bucks Tech Help can also design around current UniFi **Wi-Fi 7 installation** hardware.

Bucks Tech Help currently lists whole-home Wi-Fi projects from £1,500. The final price depends on the property or business layout, number of access points, cable routes, switching and any garden-building or multi-floor requirements. A quote audit and survey are used to scope the installation.

### Do you install Wi-Fi for Aylesbury businesses as well as homes?

Yes. Bucks Tech Help installs hardwired UniFi Wi-Fi for homes as well as suitable offices, clinics, retail premises, warehouses and commercial units in Aylesbury.

### Can you improve Wi-Fi in a new-build Aylesbury home?

Yes. A hardwired design can place access points around the finished layout so upper floors, home offices and rear rooms do not have to depend on one router. Where suitable cable routes exist, access points connect back to the network over Ethernet.
